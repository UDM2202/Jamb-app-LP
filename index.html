The problem

Concrete design in practice is still spread across spreadsheets, hand calculations and desktop packages that return an answer without showing how it was reached. That is a real difficulty for the engineer who has to sign the drawing, and a larger one for the engineer who has to check it. A result is only useful if the working behind it can be read, challenged and defended.

What we built

StructAI is a browser based design environment for reinforced concrete elements. An engineer describes the member, its loads and its support conditions, and receives a complete design: analysis, section sizing, reinforcement, serviceability checks and a calculation report that cites the governing Eurocode clause for every step. Nothing is installed and nothing is hidden.

The platform currently covers nine design modules:

  • One way slabs
  • Two way slabs
  • Continuous slabs
  • Simply supported beams
  • Continuous beams
  • Columns
  • Pad footings
  • Combined footings
  • Staircases

Inside the engine

The analysis and design engine is our own work, written in Python and exposed through a typed API. It runs to more than eight thousand lines of solver and code checking logic, and it was developed against worked examples and the corrections of practising engineers.

  • Analysis. Continuous members are solved with a linear elastic finite element model and a banded symmetric solver, with pattern loading applied automatically. The report exposes the analysis internals so a checker can follow the moments back to the stiffness model.
  • Columns. Braced and unbraced members, slenderness and second order effects, biaxial bending, sub frame analysis, and load take down from tributary areas and floor loads when the axial force is not known in advance.
  • Foundations. Pad footings and combined footings for any number of columns, including pedestals, eccentric columns, water table effects, bearing pressure and punching shear.
  • Serviceability and detailing. Deflection, crack control, cover for exposure and fire, anchorage, and bar selection that respects the sizes the engineer prefers to use on site.
  • Cost and alternatives. Each design is priced from a regional rates database (United Kingdom and Nigeria today) and presented beside alternative sections, so the trade between depth, steel and cost is visible before a decision is made.

Decisions that mattered

The engineer's input is law. If a user fixes the cover, the effective depth or the bar diameter, the engine designs around that choice and says so in the report. It never substitutes a value quietly. This sounds obvious and is routinely violated by design software.

Capacity is checked twice. Section moment capacity is computed with the rectangular stress block, then verified by an independent routine that integrates the parabola rectangle law across four hundred layers. Two different stress models, two different integrations, one answer. Contract tests hold the interface and the engine to the same schema so a change on one side cannot silently break the other.

The report is the product. Every design exports a calculation document with a cover page, clause references, shear force and bending moment diagrams, deflection plots and reinforcement details, in PDF and CSV. It is written to be submitted, not just read on screen.

Around the calculation

Engineering is done by teams, so the platform includes organisations, workspaces, projects, role based permissions, comments on designs and project chat. A quick design mode serves the engineer who needs one element checked in five minutes, and a companion desktop agent links the platform to external design tools.

The problem

Every year a very large number of Nigerian students sit the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination, and a single score decides which university course is open to them. The exam is computer based and strictly timed. Many candidates meet that format for the first time on the day, having prepared from printed past papers that give an answer key and no explanation.

What we built

PrepJ puts the whole preparation in one place and makes it free to start. A candidate chooses a department (Science, Art or Commercial), then Use of English and the three subjects in their combination, and begins practising within minutes on whatever device they own.

  • Past questions by year and topic. Questions are organised by subject, topic and exam year, so revision can be aimed at a weak area and not spread thin across a syllabus.
  • Timed CBT mock exams. A mock reproduces the pace and layout of the real computer based test, then returns a score and the topics that cost the most marks.
  • An explanation for every answer. The candidate sees why an option is correct, not only that it is.
  • AI tutor. When an explanation is not enough, the student can ask the tutor to work through the idea again in plainer terms, at any hour.
  • Progress, weak topics and rankings. Scores are tracked over time, weak topics are surfaced, and a ranking shows where a candidate stands among others preparing for the same paper.

Decisions that mattered

The web came first. A student should not need storage space, a recent phone or an app store account to begin. The web application works on any phone, tablet or computer today, and the native apps follow from the same foundation.

Structure before volume. A question bank is only as useful as its indexing. Every question carries its subject, topic and year, which is what makes targeted practice, weak topic detection and honest progress reporting possible.

The tutor explains, it does not replace study. The AI tutor is placed after the attempt, where it can address a specific misunderstanding. The student still does the work, and that is the point.

Cybersecurity

Find the weakness before someone else does, then close it properly.

We build software for a living, which shapes how we secure it. We know where developers cut corners under deadline, how a session token ends up in a log file, and why the permission check that exists on the page is missing from the API behind it. Our security work starts from that knowledge.

Every engagement is agreed in writing before it starts: scope, method, testing window and rules of engagement. Findings are reproduced, rated by real business impact and delivered with a fix a developer can apply. Then we test again to confirm the fix holds.

  1. ScopeAgree targets, limits and authorisation in writing.
  2. AssessTest manually and with tooling, as an attacker would.
  3. ReportDeliver evidence, impact and a prioritised remedy for each finding.
  4. RetestVerify the fixes and record what was closed.

  • Penetration testing

    Web applications, mobile apps, APIs and networks tested against the ways they are actually attacked: broken access control, injection, authentication and session flaws, insecure configuration and business logic abuse.

  • Vulnerability assessment

    A structured sweep of your external and internal estate, with false positives removed and findings ordered by the risk they carry, so effort goes where it counts.

  • Secure code and architecture review

    We read the code and the design. Threat modelling, review of authentication, authorisation, secrets handling, cryptography and data flows, before launch or after an incident.

  • Cloud and infrastructure hardening

    Identity and access review, network segmentation, encryption, backup integrity, patching and logging, brought to a documented baseline your team can maintain.

  • Monitoring and incident readiness

    Logging that answers questions, alerting that is worth waking up for, and a response plan that has been rehearsed before it is needed.

  • Data protection and staff awareness

    Practical preparation for data protection obligations, including the Nigeria Data Protection Act, and training that teaches people to recognise phishing and social engineering in the form it really takes.

Security is also built into everything we ship. This site, for example, is served under a strict content security policy, loads no third party scripts and sets no tracking cookies.
